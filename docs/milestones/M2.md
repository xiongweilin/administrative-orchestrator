# M2 — organizational authority and policy

Status: completed reference baseline

M2 turned the onboarding reference slice from a durable execution demonstration into an organization-governed automation system. Its accepted baseline is preserved by CI and extended, not redefined, by M3 administrative correctness.

## Objective achieved

The M2 system durably answers:

1. who submitted or changed a request;
2. which facts and policy version were current;
3. which organizational roles were required to decide;
4. whether authenticated decision makers were eligible at decision time;
5. whether multi-party / separation-of-duties requirements were satisfied;
6. exactly what approval satisfaction supported governed execution;
7. what effects crossed the current reality boundary;
8. what external postconditions were observed;
9. why the case was considered complete, waiting, failed, or reopened.

## Preserved invariants

```text
Authentication != authority
RoleAssignment != current role eligibility
Decision != ApprovalSatisfaction
ApprovalSatisfaction != ExecutionAuthorization
Policy definition != PolicyEvaluation
Effect success != ConfirmedOutcome
Object existence != semantic verification
ConfirmedOutcome set != completion unless the completion contract is satisfied
```

Authority-sensitive case changes advance `authority_epoch`; old decisions, approval satisfactions, and execution authorizations cannot silently survive a changed case-local fact/policy world.

## Accepted reference implementation

The employee-onboarding slice demonstrates:

- authenticated requester identity;
- durable source-event idempotency;
- immutable fact history and current-fact replacement;
- persisted versioned onboarding policy;
- standard HR approval;
- privileged onboarding requiring manager + access approver with distinct principals;
- scoped role assignments and bounded delegation;
- durable `ApprovalSatisfaction` containing the supporting decision set;
- approval-backed governed execution;
- provider-side effect idempotency;
- unknown-outcome reconciliation without blind resend;
- semantic read-back verification;
- explicit completion assessment;
- durable audit/inspection;
- PostgreSQL/DBOS worker restart recovery;
- full Compose E2E for standard and privileged onboarding.

## M3 handoff

M2 intentionally did not solve several correctness problems that become mandatory before kernel cutover or production connectors:

- authenticated user != arbitrary resource permission;
- case-local `authority_epoch` cannot detect independent role/delegation/policy drift by itself;
- completion must originate from business obligations rather than the effect plan;
- authoritative ABSENT must remain distinct from UNAVAILABLE/UNKNOWN/STALE observation;
- request claims and human attestations must be distinguished from authoritative facts;
- policy lifecycle changes require explicit actor/reason and immutable version definitions.

Those concerns are owned by **M3 — administrative correctness**, documented in `docs/architecture.md` and ADR 0001. M3 does not erase or reinterpret accepted M2 history.

## Runtime boundaries retained

`AdministrativeCase` owns administrative business state. DBOS owns durable orchestration only. The Policy Plane owns persisted policy versions. The Authority Plane owns principals, current role/delegation qualification and approval satisfaction. External systems own realized business state.

Stage M4 will converge physical execution on `agent-kernel`; M2/M3 administrative business semantics remain upstream of that runtime boundary.
