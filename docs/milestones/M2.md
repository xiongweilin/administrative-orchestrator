# M2 — organizational authority and policy

M2 turns the onboarding reference slice from a durable execution demonstration into an organization-governed automation system.

## Objective

A successful M2 system can answer, durably and audibly:

1. who submitted or changed a request;
2. which facts and policy version were current;
3. which organizational roles were required to decide;
4. whether the authenticated decision makers were eligible at decision time;
5. whether multi-party / separation-of-duties requirements were satisfied;
6. exactly what execution authority was derived from that approval world;
7. what effects crossed the reality boundary;
8. what external postconditions were actually observed;
9. why the case was considered complete, waiting, failed, or reopened.

## Required invariants

```text
Authentication != authority
RoleAssignment != current role eligibility
Decision != ApprovalSatisfaction
ApprovalSatisfaction != ExecutionAuthorization
Policy definition != PolicyEvaluation
Effect success != ConfirmedOutcome
Object existence != semantic verification
ConfirmedOutcome set != completion unless the completion contract is satisfied
```

Authority-sensitive changes advance `authority_epoch`; old decisions, approval satisfactions, and execution authorizations cannot silently survive a changed fact/policy world.

## Reference implementation scope

The employee-onboarding slice must demonstrate:

- authenticated requester identity;
- durable source-event idempotency;
- immutable fact history and current-fact replacement;
- persisted versioned onboarding policy;
- standard HR approval;
- privileged onboarding requiring manager + access approver with distinct principals;
- scoped role assignments and bounded delegation;
- durable `ApprovalSatisfaction` containing the supporting decision set;
- `ExecutionAuthorization` backed by current approval satisfaction in the governed deployment;
- provider-side effect idempotency;
- unknown-outcome reconciliation without blind resend;
- semantic read-back verification;
- explicit completion assessment;
- durable audit and operations inspection;
- worker/process restart recovery.

## Runtime boundaries

`AdministrativeCase` owns the administrative business state machine.

DBOS owns durable waiting, replay, resume, and wake-up. It must not redefine administrative policy, authority, effect, or completion semantics.

The Policy Plane owns persisted policy versions. Deterministic evaluator code compiles and evaluates those records but is not the policy source of truth by itself.

The Authority Plane owns principals, identity bindings, current role eligibility, delegation, and approval satisfaction. Caller-provided strings do not establish authority.

External systems remain authoritative for realized business state. `ConfirmedOutcome` requires fresh evidence from the reality boundary and domain semantic verification.

## Acceptance

M2 is accepted when CI proves all of the following on the reference slice:

- unit/semantic tests pass;
- Alembic upgrade -> downgrade -> upgrade succeeds on SQLite and PostgreSQL;
- DBOS restart recovery completes without duplicate effects;
- full Compose E2E rejects unauthenticated access;
- an ineligible requester cannot forge HR approval;
- normal onboarding authorizes after one current HR approval;
- privileged onboarding remains blocked after only manager approval;
- privileged onboarding authorizes only after a distinct access approver also approves;
- governed execution persists approval-backed authorization, effects, semantic realizations, confirmed outcomes, and completion evidence.

## Explicitly out of M2

M2 does not claim production completeness for:

- enterprise OIDC/JWKS/key rotation;
- HRIS/IAM/ERP production connectors;
- Policy Plane authoring UI, staged rollout, simulation, or rollback operations;
- natural-language/LLM request interpretation;
- broad administrative workflow coverage;
- full human exception operations console;
- cross-domain orchestration.

Those capabilities should be added only by consuming the M2 distinctions rather than bypassing them.
